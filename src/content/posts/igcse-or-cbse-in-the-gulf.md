Many Indian families moving to the Gulf face the same decision: stay with CBSE, or switch to an international board such as IGCSE? Both are respected, and schools across the UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman offer them. The right choice depends less on which board is “better” and more on your child and your family’s plans.

## The two boards at a glance

**CBSE (Central Board of Secondary Education)** is India’s national board. Teaching is built around NCERT textbooks, with board exams at the end of Class 10 and Class 12. The syllabus aligns closely with major Indian entrance exams.

**IGCSE (International General Certificate of Secondary Education)** is offered by international exam boards such as Cambridge and Pearson Edexcel, usually taken at the end of Grades 9–10. It offers wide subject choice and an international outlook, and students often continue to A Levels or the IB Diploma afterwards.

## How they differ in the classroom

- **Approach:** CBSE covers a broad, structured syllabus with strong emphasis on concepts and practice. IGCSE tends to emphasise application, analysis and extended writing.
- **Assessment:** CBSE relies mainly on board exams, with some internal assessment. IGCSE is assessed mainly through final exams, and some subjects include coursework or practical components.
- **Subject choice:** IGCSE generally allows more flexibility in choosing subjects and levels. CBSE follows a more fixed combination.
- **Textbooks:** CBSE centres on NCERT. IGCSE uses board-endorsed textbooks and past papers.

## Questions to ask before deciding

1. **Where will your family be in three to five years?** If a return to India is likely, staying with CBSE usually makes the transition smoother.
2. **What are your child’s long-term plans?** Engineering or medicine in India points towards CBSE. Plans to study abroad may make an international pathway attractive.
3. **Which schools are practical for you?** Fees, location and seat availability often narrow the choice.
4. **How does your child learn best?** Some children thrive with structure and practice; others enjoy open-ended projects and discussion.
5. **When would the switch happen?** Changing boards is easier in the earlier grades than close to exams.

## If your child is switching boards

A change of board can leave gaps — topics taught in a different order, new question styles, or unfamiliar assessment formats. Common challenges include:

- Adjusting to longer, application-based answers (moving to IGCSE).
- Covering a wider syllabus at a faster pace (moving to CBSE).
- Learning new exam techniques and marking expectations.

A tutor who knows **both** boards can map what your child has already covered, fill the gaps and build confidence with the new format — often within a few weeks.

## The bottom line

There is no single right answer. Choose the board that fits your family’s plans and your child’s way of learning — and give them support during the transition.
