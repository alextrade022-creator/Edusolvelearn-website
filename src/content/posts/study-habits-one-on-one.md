One-on-one tuition gives your child a teacher’s full attention. But what happens between classes matters just as much as the class itself. These five habits help turn good lessons into lasting progress.

## 1. Arrive with questions

The biggest advantage of one-on-one teaching is that the class can follow your child’s needs. That only works if the tutor knows what those needs are.

Encourage your child to keep a small “doubts notebook” during the week. Whenever something in school or homework is confusing, they write it down. At the start of each class, they share the list. Ten minutes on real doubts often achieves more than an hour on a new topic.

## 2. Review within a day

We forget new material quickly unless we revisit it. A short review within 24 hours of a class — just 10 to 15 minutes — helps the lesson stick.

A simple routine: re-read the class notes, redo one or two problems from the lesson, and write a three-line summary of what was learned.

## 3. Keep a fixed study spot

A consistent place to study tells the brain it’s time to focus. It doesn’t need to be fancy:

- A quiet corner with a table and good light.
- Books, notebook and stationery within reach.
- The phone kept away, or on silent, during study time.

For online classes, this spot also means a stable internet connection and headphones — so the class starts on time, without distractions.

## 4. Short, regular practice

Twenty focused minutes every day beats a long session once a week. Regular practice builds memory and confidence steadily, and it prevents the stress of last-minute cramming before tests.

Help your child set a small daily target — a few Maths problems, a page of reading, five new words — and keep it going even on busy days.

## 5. Share school updates with the tutor

Tuition works best when it stays in step with school. Make sure the tutor knows about:

- Upcoming tests and exams.
- Homework your child is finding difficult.
- Topics the class will cover next.
- Feedback or marks from school.

This lets the tutor prepare your child for what’s coming, rather than reacting after a difficult test.

## Bringing it together

None of these habits takes much time. Together, they help your child arrive at each class ready to learn, remember more afterwards, and grow more confident week by week.
